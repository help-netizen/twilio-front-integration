Albusto · ESTIMATE-REDESIGN-001 · v2

Estimates, rebuilt around getting a yes

An invoice asks to be paid; an estimate asks to be agreed to. So the loudest thing is where the proposal stands on the way to an answer, and the primary button is always the next move toward it. UX follows the shipped invoice rebuild — one scroll, one ✕, big buttons, no kebab. Type follows the payment card: 32 / 20 / 15, with weight and colour doing the rest.

One skeleton, every card. The first pass drew each screen on its own, so the screens disagreed: the summary appeared in the editor and vanished from the detail, and the contact was promoted to a section with an icon — which fairly invites the question of why the job did not get one too. An estimate has three layers, and they are always in this order:
  1. Identity — the amount, the status, and one quiet line naming who it is for and what it belongs to. The context is a line, never a section.
  2. The document — Summary, Items, Total. It is what the customer agrees to, so it appears on every surface that shows the estimate, including the customer's own page.
  3. The record — History, at the very bottom, in grey. System, not document.
Actions sit between identity and the document: you usually arrive knowing what you came to do.

S5 · Editor
The generate card is kept exactly as the app ships it — lavender panel, its own heading, a real text area, Generate. It is large on purpose: this is the fastest way to build an estimate, and it should look like an offer rather than a hint you can skip. Only the words changed: it does not require a formal report, so it no longer says one. Say what the job is in your own words and it writes the summary and the lines below it — which is why it sits above them.
